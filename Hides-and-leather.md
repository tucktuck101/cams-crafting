## Shadowhide

**Rarity:** Uncommon

Shadowhide is created by curing ordinary leather in magical darkness and treating it with specially prepared oils that react to the absence of light. The leather is repeatedly exposed to areas where mundane and magical shadows overlap, allowing the darkness to gradually become bound within its fibres.

Shadowhide is usually black, charcoal grey, or exceptionally dark brown. Unlike ordinary black leather, however, it seems to absorb surrounding light rather than reflect it. Its surface has a soft, almost velvety appearance, and its edges can appear slightly blurred when viewed in dim light.

The effect becomes particularly noticeable when the leather enters darkness. Colours seem to disappear from its surface, allowing Shadowhide to blend naturally into surrounding shadows. In complete darkness, it can become surprisingly difficult to distinguish from the space around it.

Shadowhide does not create darkness itself. Instead, it responds to darkness that already exists. Bright light reveals the material more clearly, while dim light allows its magical properties to become increasingly pronounced. This makes it especially valuable for armour, cloaks, gloves, boots, and other equipment used by scouts, thieves, spies, and adventurers who regularly operate at night.

The curing process is delicate. The leather must be exposed to magical darkness without allowing it to become completely saturated, as excessive exposure can cause the material to lose its flexibility and become unnaturally cold. Properly prepared Shadowhide remains supple and comfortable despite its connection to darkness.

Because its magic comes from the crafting process rather than its source material, almost any suitable leather can be transformed into Shadowhide. Different tanning traditions produce slightly different shades and textures, but the characteristic light-absorbing quality remains.

### Light Armour Effect

**Umbral Camouflage.** You have advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made while in dim light or darkness.

**Shadowstep.** As a bonus action while in dim light or darkness, you can become shrouded in supernatural shadow until the start of your next turn. During this time, you can take the Hide action while only lightly obscured.

Once you use Shadowstep, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Umbral Camouflage.** You have advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made while in dim light or darkness.

**Veil of Shadows.** When a creature you can see makes an attack against you while you are in dim light or darkness, you can use your reaction to impose disadvantage on the attack roll.

Once you use Veil of Shadows, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

## Skysteel

**Rarity:** Uncommon

Skysteel is a magical leather created by incorporating finely powdered meteoric iron into the tanning process and repeatedly exposing the leather to powerful winds during its preparation. The leather is stretched, treated, and worked while under the influence of magical air currents, causing the fibres to become unusually light without sacrificing their strength.

The finished material is remarkably thin and lightweight. Its colour varies depending on the original leather, though grey, blue-grey, and pale brown are common. Fine metallic flecks can sometimes be seen within the grain, giving the surface a subtle sheen reminiscent of a cloudy sky. When held loosely, Skysteel can flutter in even the slightest breeze.

Despite its lightness, Skysteel is surprisingly durable. It flexes rather than resisting movement, allowing armour made from it to move naturally with its wearer. The material seems to respond particularly well to sudden changes in direction, making it popular among scouts, rangers, messengers, climbers, and anyone who values freedom of movement.

Skysteel also interacts strangely with falling air. A piece dropped from a height descends more slowly than expected, sometimes drifting sideways before reaching the ground. Skilled craftsmen can exploit this property when constructing armour, cloaks, climbing equipment, and other gear intended for those who frequently travel through mountainous or elevated terrain.

The meteoric iron used in its creation does not need to be particularly pure or valuable. What matters is the magical treatment during the crafting process. The leather must be worked outdoors or within a magically controlled wind chamber, making production difficult in calm or enclosed environments.

### Light Armour Effect

**Light as Air.** Your walking speed increases by 10 feet.

**Windborne.** You have advantage on saving throws against being knocked prone or forcibly moved.

You also reduce any falling damage you take by 20.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Light as Air.** Your walking speed increases by 5 feet.

**Windborne.** You have advantage on saving throws against being knocked prone or forcibly moved.

**Controlled Descent.** When you fall, you can use your reaction to slow your descent. You take no falling damage and can move horizontally up to 10 feet for every 30 feet you descend.

## Fireglass

**Rarity:** Uncommon

Fireglass is a magical leather created through a demanding process of heat treatment and alchemical curing. Ordinary leather is repeatedly exposed to intense magical flames before being cooled in specially prepared baths containing powdered volcanic glass and heat-resistant minerals. The process is repeated several times, gradually changing the structure of the leather without making it brittle.

Finished Fireglass is usually dark red, burnt orange, or deep brown, with a faint glass-like sheen across its surface. Along the edges and thinner portions of the leather, the material can appear almost translucent, as though light is passing through a very thin layer of coloured glass. Despite its appearance, Fireglass remains soft and flexible, bending naturally with the wearer.

Fireglass retains heat remarkably well. A piece left beside a fire becomes warm but does not scorch, and the material can remain warm for hours after being removed from a heat source. When exposed to flames, faint orange lines sometimes appear beneath its surface, following the grain of the leather like cracks in heated glass.

The crafting process makes Fireglass highly resistant to ordinary fire. This makes it particularly valuable for protective clothing, armour, gloves, aprons, and equipment intended for smiths, alchemists, and adventurers who frequently work around extreme heat.

Creating Fireglass requires considerable skill. Too much heat destroys the leather, while insufficient heat prevents the volcanic glass from properly binding with the material. As a result, Fireglass is uncommon despite requiring no inherently magical creature or substance to produce.

### Light Armour Effect

**Fireproof.** You have resistance to fire damage.

**Heat Shield.** When you take fire damage, you can use your reaction to reduce the damage by 1d8 + your Proficiency Bonus.

Once you use Heat Shield, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Fireproof.** You have resistance to fire damage.

**Flareback.** When a creature within 5 feet of you hits you with a melee attack, you can use your reaction to deal 2d6 fire damage to the attacker.

Once you use Flareback, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

## Tidehide

**Rarity:** Uncommon

Tidehide is a magical leather created by repeatedly soaking ordinary leather in magically purified seawater before curing it with salt, powdered pearl, and water-aspected reagents. The leather is worked while submerged, with the process repeated until the material develops a natural affinity for water.

Finished Tidehide is usually blue-grey, sea-green, or deep brown, with a subtle sheen that makes its surface appear perpetually damp. Despite this appearance, it never becomes waterlogged or heavy. Water rolls across the leather in smooth beads and leaves it almost immediately.

The magical treatment makes Tidehide remarkably flexible. The leather seems to flow around the wearer rather than resist their movements, and sudden changes in pressure or direction barely affect it. Properly crafted Tidehide is particularly prized by sailors, divers, explorers, and adventurers who frequently travel through aquatic environments.

### Light Armour Effect

**Tideborne.** You gain a swimming speed equal to your walking speed, and you have advantage on ability checks made to swim or resist being swept away by water.

**Water's Grace.** When you are knocked prone, you can use your reaction to immediately stand without spending movement. You can then move up to 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks.

Once you use Water's Grace, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Tideborne.** You gain a swimming speed equal to your walking speed, and you have advantage on ability checks made to swim or resist being swept away by water.

**Undertow.** When a creature within 5 feet of you hits you with a melee attack, you can use your reaction to force it to make a DC 15 Strength saving throw. On a failed save, the creature is pushed up to 15 feet away from you.

Once you use Undertow, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

## Voidleather

**Rarity:** Rare

Voidleather is created through a painstaking process in which ordinary leather is treated with rare spatial reagents and repeatedly exposed to magically distorted spaces. The leather must be stretched across a carefully prepared planar frame while spells that manipulate distance and position are woven through its fibres. The technique is known among a small number of specialist leatherworkers and practitioners of spatial magic, although the equipment and reagents required make the process expensive.

Finished Voidleather is unnaturally dark, ranging from deep charcoal to absolute black. Its surface appears smooth but never quite seems to occupy the same position twice. When viewed from an angle, its edges can appear slightly displaced from where they actually are, while objects reflected in its surface sometimes appear a fraction of an inch away from their true position.

The material is extraordinarily light for its strength. More strangely, the amount of leather used does not always seem to correspond perfectly to the amount of space it occupies. A folded piece may appear slightly larger on the inside than its exterior would suggest, while armour crafted from it can feel almost weightless when worn.

Voidleather interacts strangely with distance. Objects held against it may seem closer or farther away than they actually are, and a hand pressed against its surface can occasionally appear to extend a tiny distance beyond where it should. These effects are harmless in properly prepared leather, but they make the material exceptionally difficult to cut, shape, and stitch.

Voidleather is prized by explorers, scouts, assassins, and spellcasters who specialise in teleportation and spatial magic. Its rarity comes not from a lost method of creation, but from the scarcity of suitable reagents, specialised equipment, and craftsmen capable of maintaining stable spatial enchantments throughout the tanning process.

### Light Armour Effect

**Spatial Distortion.** You can take the Disengage action as a bonus action.

**Fold Space.** As a bonus action, you can teleport to an unoccupied space you can see within 30 feet. This movement does not provoke opportunity attacks.

You can use Fold Space three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Spatial Distortion.** You can take the Disengage action as a bonus action.

**Voidstep.** As a reaction when you are hit by an attack, you can teleport to an unoccupied space you can see within 30 feet. The attack misses you as you disappear.

You can use Voidstep three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

## Angelforged Leather

**Rarity:** Rare

Angelforged Leather is created by treating ordinary leather with powdered precious metals, sanctified oils, and concentrated radiant magic. The leather is repeatedly exposed to intense magical radiance while being worked, causing its fibres to harden and become exceptionally resistant without losing their flexibility. The process is well understood by experienced craftsmen, but requires specialised materials and access to sufficiently powerful sources of radiant magic.

The finished material is usually pale gold, ivory, cream, or warm bronze. Fine patterns resembling feathers, rays of sunlight, or delicate wings naturally appear within the grain as the leather is treated. In darkness, these patterns can emit a faint golden glow. No two pieces develop the same markings, and master armourers often consider particularly intricate patterns a sign of exceptional craftsmanship.

Angelforged Leather is remarkably resilient. Blades slide across its surface with unusual difficulty, while impacts that would normally tear or puncture ordinary leather often leave only shallow marks. Despite this strength, the material remains supple and comfortable enough for light and medium armour, making it particularly valuable to warriors who require protection without the weight or rigidity of heavier materials.

The radiant energy bound within Angelforged Leather reacts instinctively to danger. When the wearer is threatened, faint warmth spreads through the material and the patterns within the leather briefly brighten. Skilled craftsmen can further shape this property into protective enchantments, allowing the leather to shield its wearer or extend its protection to nearby allies.

The creation of Angelforged Leather is closely associated with temples, celestial scholars, and master armourers. It does not require the involvement of an actual celestial creature; however, the crafting process must be performed in the presence of concentrated positive or radiant magic. Because suitable radiant sources and sanctified materials are not readily available to ordinary craftsmen, production is limited to certain temples, specialist workshops, and regions with established celestial traditions.

### Light Armour Effect

**Radiant Protection.** You have resistance to radiant damage and have advantage on saving throws against being blinded or frightened.

**Guardian's Light.** When you or a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to reduce the damage by 3d8 + your Proficiency Bonus.

If this reduces the damage to 0, the protected creature instead gains temporary hit points equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

You can use Guardian's Light three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Radiant Protection.** You have resistance to radiant damage and have advantage on saving throws against being blinded or frightened.

**Celestial Bulwark.** When you or a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to grant that creature resistance to the triggering damage, potentially reducing the damage by half.

You can use Celestial Bulwark three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

## Gravemantle

**Rarity:** Rare

Gravemantle is created by curing ordinary leather in a mixture of grave soil, funerary herbs, powdered bone, and specially prepared alchemical oils. During the final stages of its creation, the leather is exposed to controlled necromantic magic, binding a fragment of the stillness associated with death into its fibres. The technique is known among certain necromancers, funerary orders, and specialist leatherworkers, although the preparation requires considerable care.

Despite its grim creation, Gravemantle is neither rotten nor unpleasant. The finished material is usually dark grey, brown, or black, with a texture resembling weathered leather. Pale lines occasionally appear through the grain, resembling old scars or cracks in dried earth. These markings often become more pronounced as the leather ages.

The defining characteristic of Gravemantle is its remarkable resistance to deterioration. It does not rot or mildew easily, and it resists tearing and other forms of physical damage. Small cuts and abrasions seem to close slowly on their own, allowing armour made from Gravemantle to remain serviceable for generations with surprisingly little maintenance.

The magic within Gravemantle gives its wearer an unusual connection to endurance and stillness. Fatigue seems to settle more slowly upon them, while injuries that would normally leave someone incapacitated can sometimes be endured through sheer force of will. The material does not restore life or prevent injury; rather, it seems reluctant to allow its wearer to simply stop.

The crafting process requires the leather to spend several nights within a prepared funerary environment, where the necromantic energy is carefully regulated. Too much magic causes the leather to become brittle and lifeless, while too little leaves it mundane. The ingredients and techniques are known, but suitable funerary sites, experienced practitioners, and properly prepared alchemical oils limit production to specialist workshops and necromantic traditions.

### Light Armour Effect

**Gravebound Vitality.** You have advantage on saving throws against exhaustion, poison, and disease, and you don't need to eat or drink.

**Refuse the Grave.** When you would be reduced to 0 hit points but not killed outright, you can use your reaction to drop to 1 hit point instead. You then gain temporary hit points equal to 2d8 + your Constitution modifier.

You can use Refuse the Grave three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Gravebound Vitality.** You have advantage on saving throws against exhaustion, poison, and disease, and you don't need to eat or drink.

**Deathless Endurance.** When you take damage that would reduce you to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to instead remain at 1 hit point and gain resistance to all damage until the end of your next turn.

You can use Deathless Endurance three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

## Abyssal Hide

**Rarity:** Rare

Abyssal Hide is created through a dangerous process that exposes ordinary hide to concentrated destructive magic. The hide is treated with alchemical reagents derived from volatile minerals and infused with controlled traces of fiendish or Abyssal energy. Unlike materials made from inherently magical creatures, the power of Abyssal Hide comes entirely from the transformation process.

The finished hide is usually deep black, dark crimson, bruised purple, or unnatural shades of blue and green. Its surface is covered in irregular patterns resembling veins, cracks, or branching scars. These markings seem to shift subtly when the hide is exposed to strong emotion or magic, occasionally brightening as though something beneath the surface is struggling to escape.

Abyssal Hide is unnaturally aggressive in the way it responds to magical energy. Spells and magical attacks striking the material can cause its markings to flare briefly, while weapons crafted from it seem to carry a faint sense of destructive potential. Properly prepared hide remains stable, however, and does not spontaneously release the energy bound within it.

The material is extremely difficult to work safely. During the crafting process, the hide must be repeatedly subjected to destructive magic without allowing that energy to overwhelm the underlying material. Poorly prepared examples can become brittle, warp unpredictably, or discharge their stored energy. Experienced armourers can reliably produce usable hide, but the process remains hazardous even in skilled hands.

Abyssal Hide is particularly associated with fiend hunters, battle-mages, mercenaries, and adventurers who value overwhelming force. Its creation is known among a limited number of alchemists, armourers, and practitioners of destructive magic, but suitable reagents and controlled Abyssal energy are difficult to acquire. This keeps Abyssal Hide uncommon even in regions where its crafting techniques are known.

### Light Armour Effect

**Abyssal Power.** Once per turn when you deal damage to a creature with an attack or spell, you can cause the hide to erupt with destructive energy. The creature takes an additional 1d8 necrotic damage.

**Ruinous Step.** As a bonus action, you can unleash the destructive energy stored within the hide, teleporting up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see. Each creature within 5 feet of the space you leave must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw, taking 3d8 necrotic damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success.

Until the start of your next turn, your movement does not provoke opportunity attacks.

You can use Ruinous Step three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Abyssal Power.** Once per turn when you deal damage to a creature with an attack or spell, you can cause the hide to erupt with destructive energy. The creature takes an additional 1d8 necrotic damage.

**Unleash the Abyss.** As an action, you release the destructive energy stored within the hide in a 15-foot-radius sphere centred on yourself. Each creature of your choice within the area must make a DC 15 Constitution saving throw, taking 6d8 necrotic damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success.

A creature that fails the saving throw also cannot regain hit points until the start of your next turn.

You can use Unleash the Abyss once, regaining the ability to do so when you finish a Long Rest.

## Dragonfire Leather

**Rarity:** Very Rare

Dragonfire Leather is created by subjecting ordinary leather to a series of magical treatments designed to reproduce the elemental forces associated with dragons. Rather than drawing its power from a particular dragon or creature, the crafting process incorporates carefully prepared reagents representing the great draconic lineages.

The finished leather can display almost any draconic colour. Chromatic colours may dominate one piece, metallic tones another, while exceptionally rare examples develop crystalline or gemstone-like patterns associated with gem dragons.

The leather responds to the wearer's will, allowing them to awaken different forms of draconic power. Its magic encompasses the destructive breath of chromatic dragons, the elemental gifts of metallic dragons, and the strange psychic power of gem dragons.

Dragonfire Leather is therefore something of a misnomer, though the name remains popular among adventurers. Scholars and master armourers generally call it “Draconic Leather.”

### Light Armour Effect: Living Dragon

**Draconic Resistance.** When you finish a Long Rest, choose one damage type from the following: Acid, Cold, Fire, Poison, Thunder, Lightning, Radiant, Force, Psychic. You have resistance to that damage type until you finish your next Long Rest.

**Draconic Step.** As a bonus action, you awaken the draconic power within the leather and teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see. Creatures within 5 feet of the space you leave and the space you arrive in must make a DC 16 Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 4d8 damage of the type you selected with Draconic Resistance, or half as much on a successful save.

You can use Draconic Step three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect: Dragon's Mantle

**Draconic Resistance.** When you finish a Long Rest, choose one damage type from the following: Acid, Cold, Fire, Poison, Thunder, Lightning, Radiant, Force, Psychic. You have resistance to that damage type until you finish your next Long Rest.

**Draconic Retaliation.** When a creature within 5 feet hits you with a melee attack, you can use your reaction to force it to make a DC 16 Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, it takes 4d8 damage of your chosen type, or half as much on a successful save.

**Unleash the Dragon.** As an action, you unleash a devastating breath of draconic energy. Each creature of your choice in a 30-foot cone must make a DC 16 Dexterity saving throw, taking 8d8 damage of your chosen type on a failed save, or half as much on a successful save.

Once you use Unleash the Dragon, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

## Starlight Leather

Starlight Leather is created through a nearly forgotten celestial tanning process in which ordinary leather is treated with silver dust, powdered crystal, and oils infused with concentrated celestial magic. The leather must then be stretched beneath an unobstructed night sky while a series of ancient rites are performed, allowing the material to absorb the light of the stars. Only a handful of leatherworkers, diviners, and celestial scholars are believed to understand the complete process.

The finished leather is usually deep blue, violet, or nearly black, scattered with tiny points of silver and white that resemble a night sky. When exposed to magic, the specks brighten and slowly shift position as though they were real stars. The patterns differ from piece to piece, and particularly skilled craftsmen claim that some resemble constellations that are not visible from the Material Plane.

Starlight Leather has an unusual relationship with concealment and revelation. It does not simply illuminate its surroundings; instead, the magical stars within the leather reveal things that ordinary light cannot easily show. Invisible creatures, magical disguises, and creatures concealed within magical darkness can sometimes appear as distorted silhouettes beneath its radiance.

The material is prized by astronomers, diviners, monster hunters, and those who hunt creatures that rely on invisibility or darkness. Its creation is exceptionally difficult because the leather must be prepared during precise celestial conditions, and the original rites have survived only in fragments. Even craftsmen who understand most of the process may be unable to reproduce a successful piece without years of study.

Most surviving examples are treated as treasures rather than commodities. A few ancient workshops are said to possess enough knowledge to create Starlight Leather, but failed attempts are common, and the necessary celestial reagents are difficult to replace. For this reason, genuine Starlight Leather is rarely encountered outside the collections of powerful scholars, temples, and experienced monster hunters.

### Light Armour Effect: Starlit Revelation

**Starborn Sight.** You can see invisible creatures and objects within 30 feet of you as faint, starry outlines, provided they are not behind total cover.

**Astral Veil.** As a bonus action, you can surround yourself with a 10-foot-radius field of starlight for 1 minute. The area is brightly lit for you and your allies and dimly lit for other creatures. Invisible creatures within the area cannot benefit from invisibility.

Once you use Astral Veil, you can't use it again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect: Celestial Bulwark

**Starborn Sight.** You can see invisible creatures and objects within 30 feet of you as faint, starry outlines, provided they are not behind total cover.

**Starlit Protection.** When you or a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to surround that creature with protective starlight. Reduce the damage by 3d10 + your proficiency bonus.

If this reduces the damage to 0, the protected creature can immediately move up to 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks.

You can use Starlit Protection three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

## Soulweave

Soulweave is created through an ancient ritual tanning process designed to bind concentrated life energy into ordinary leather. The leather is soaked in alchemical oils prepared from rare herbs, powdered gemstones, and specially prepared life-essence reagents before being repeatedly exposed to powerful healing magic. The basic principles of the process survive among a handful of master healers and life-magic practitioners, but successfully producing Soulweave requires considerable skill and carefully prepared materials.

The resulting leather is soft and warm, with fine golden, crimson, or pale blue threads visible throughout its surface. These threads brighten whenever the wearer or someone nearby is injured, sometimes pulsing like a heartbeat. The material remains warm even in cold environments, and prolonged contact can produce a faint sensation of living warmth beneath the fingers.

Soulweave has a remarkable tendency to respond to suffering. Its magic does not simply protect its wearer; it can draw upon the wearer's own vitality and redirect that strength toward another creature in need. This makes it particularly valuable to healers, guardians, and those who place themselves between danger and their companions.

Experienced healers sometimes describe Soulweave as “a second heartbeat.” The material seems to recognise injury almost instinctively, reacting before its wearer has consciously decided to intervene. The strongest examples can endure repeated use without losing their responsiveness, although their magical threads may temporarily fade after drawing heavily upon the wearer's vitality.

The final stage of creation is what makes Soulweave exceptionally difficult to produce. Several living participants must willingly contribute a portion of their own vitality during the ritual, allowing their life energy to become part of the leather's magical structure. No creature needs to die, but the process cannot be completed entirely through alchemy or healing magic. Finding willing participants, surviving the ritual's demands, and correctly binding the contributed vitality are skills known to only a small number of practitioners, making genuine Soulweave exceedingly uncommon.

### Light Armour Effect: Shared Vitality

**Soulbound Resilience.** You have advantage on saving throws against effects that would reduce your hit point maximum or cause you to gain a level of exhaustion.

**Share the Burden.** When a creature you can see within 30 feet takes damage, you can use your reaction to take up to half of that damage yourself. The protected creature takes the remaining damage.

Immediately after using Share the Burden, you or the protected creature can regain 3d8 + your proficiency bonus hit points.

You can use Share the Burden three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect: Soulguard

**Soulbound Resilience.** You have advantage on saving throws against effects that would reduce your hit point maximum or cause you to gain a level of exhaustion.

**Soulguard.** When you or a creature you can see within 30 feet would be reduced to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to cause that creature to drop to 1 hit point instead.

The creature then gains temporary hit points equal to 3d10 + your proficiency bonus.

You can use Soulguard three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

## Fateskin

**Rarity:** Legendary

Fateskin is among the most extraordinary magical leathers ever produced. Its creation is tied to an ancient ritual known as the Binding of Possibilities, a process so old that no living craftsman possesses the complete knowledge required to perform it. Fragments survive among master leatherworkers, fatebinders, and occult scholars, but attempts to reconstruct the ritual have produced only strange imitations.

The Binding of Possibilities requires the leather to be exposed to numerous magical effects simultaneously—some beneficial, some harmful, and some deliberately uncertain. Through powerful divination magic, the ritual forces the leather to retain multiple possible outcomes at once. The exact method by which this is accomplished has been lost.

Genuine Fateskin is supple and remarkably durable, with fine threads of silver, gold, and black running through its surface. These threads shift constantly, forming patterns resembling branching roads or countless paths diverging from a single point. The leather sometimes produces brief sensations of events that might occur, but it does not grant true foresight. Instead, it seems to sense surrounding possibilities and favour one outcome over another.

Most modern attempts produce only unstable imitations. Genuine Fateskin has been confirmed in only a handful of surviving pieces, all believed to predate the loss of the original ritual. Scholars distinguish true Fateskin through a dangerous divination test known as the Unchosen Path, in which the leather can produce physical evidence of events that almost happened—objects appearing where they were never placed, writing that was never written, or other remnants of rejected possibilities.

Because no living craftsman can reliably reproduce genuine Fateskin, each surviving piece is treated as both a priceless magical material and a fragment of a lost tradition.

Among fatebinders, an old saying remains:

> “Leather does not determine fate. It merely gives fate another path.”

### Light Armour Effect

**Fateful Instinct**

- You have advantage on initiative rolls.
- You cannot be surprised while conscious.

**Twist of Fate**

- Three times per Long Rest, when you or a creature you can see within 30 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can use your reaction to alter the outcome.
- Roll a d20 and replace the original roll with the new result.
- You must use this ability after seeing the original roll but before knowing whether it succeeds or fails.

**Impossible Escape**

- Once per Long Rest, when you are hit by an attack or fail a Dexterity saving throw, you can use your reaction to step into another possible outcome.
- You become invisible and immune to damage until the end of the current turn and can immediately move up to half your speed without provoking opportunity attacks.
- The triggering attack or effect is treated as though it never affected you.

**Fortune's Favour**

- When you finish a Long Rest, roll four d20s and record the results. You can use these rolls before your next Long Rest.
- Whenever you or a creature you can see within 60 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can replace the roll with one of your recorded rolls.
- You must decide to replace the roll before the roll's outcome is determined.
- You can choose to use a recorded roll after seeing the d20 roll but before knowing whether it succeeds or fails.
- Each recorded roll can be used only once, and you cannot use more than one recorded roll on the same d20 roll.
- Any unused recorded rolls disappear when you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect

**Fateful Instinct**

- You have advantage on initiative rolls.
- You cannot be surprised while conscious.

**Twist of Fate**

- Three times per Long Rest, when you or a creature you can see within 30 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can use your reaction to alter the outcome.
- Roll a d20 and replace the original roll with the new result.
- You must use this ability after seeing the original roll but before knowing whether it succeeds or fails.

**Rewrite Destiny**

- Once per Long Rest, when you or a creature you can see within 60 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can use your reaction to alter its fate.
- You can declare the roll a success if it failed, or a failure if it succeeded.
- You must use this ability after seeing the roll but before its consequences occur.
- You do not need to know the creature's modifiers or the DC of the check.

**Fortune's Protection**

- Once per Long Rest, when you would be reduced to 0 hit points, you can use your reaction to refuse that outcome.
- Instead, you are reduced to 1 hit point, and you can immediately move up to half your speed without provoking opportunity attacks.
- The attack or effect that reduced you to 0 hit points still occurs, but you survive through a favourable turn of fate.

## Elderbeast Hide

Elderbeast Hide is an ancient magical leather associated with the oldest forces of the natural world. Despite its name, it is not taken from any particular creature. Instead, genuine Elderbeast Hide is created through the Rite of First Hunt, an ancient ritual whose complete form has been lost to time. Only fragments of the tradition survive among a handful of druids, primal shamans, and master leatherworkers.

The original rite required materials representing the greatest qualities of living creatures—fangs, claws, feathers, scales, shed fur, blood, and other remnants gathered from powerful beasts. These were incorporated into an elaborate process of tanning and ritual magic lasting several weeks. The final stage, known as the Awakening of the Elder Form, infused the leather with the accumulated strength, resilience, speed, and instincts of countless forms of life.

Finished Elderbeast Hide is extraordinarily supple and strong. Its appearance can subtly change according to the wearer's movements or surroundings, developing patterns resembling scales, fur, feathers, or hardened hide before returning to its original form. The greatest examples seem almost alive, tightening around the wearer moments before danger and shifting to provide protection without conscious command.

No surviving craftsman knows how to create genuine Elderbeast Hide. Modern attempts to reproduce the Rite of First Hunt can produce powerful primal leathers, but none have demonstrated the same ability to adapt to their wearer. The few genuine pieces known to exist are believed to have been created during the ancient age when the original rite was still understood.

Among the oldest druids, Elderbeast Hide is described not as the hide of an elder creature, but as the memory of survival itself.

> “The beast is not within the hide. The beast is within the memory of life.”

### Light Armour Effect: Primal Instinct

**Primal Senses.** You gain darkvision out to 60 feet, or your existing darkvision increases by 60 feet. You also have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing or smell.

**Primal Adaptation.** When you finish a Long Rest, choose one of the following adaptations. You gain its benefits until you finish your next Long Rest:

- **Predator:** You have advantage on initiative rolls, and once on each of your turns when you hit a creature that is missing any hit points, you deal an additional 1d12 damage of the weapon's normal damage type.
- **Thick Hide:** Your hit point maximum increases by an amount equal to twice your proficiency bonus, and you have advantage on Constitution saving throws.
- **Apex:** You have advantage on Strength and Dexterity checks, and your carrying capacity is doubled.
- **Feral Mind:** You have advantage on Wisdom and Charisma saving throws, and you cannot be frightened while conscious.
- **Keen Senses:** You have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) and Wisdom (Survival) checks, and you gain proficiency in one skill of your choice if you aren't already proficient in it.
- **Adaptive Physiology:** Choose acid, cold, fire, lightning, or poison. You have resistance to the chosen damage type.

**Elder Reflexes.** When you fail a Dexterity saving throw, you can use your reaction to reroll it. You must use the new roll.

You can use Elder Reflexes three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

**Primal Awakening.** As a bonus action, you awaken the Elder Form within the hide for 1 minute. For the duration, you gain advantage on Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution saving throws, and you cannot be frightened, poisoned, or restrained.

Once you use Primal Awakening, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

### Medium Armour Effect: Primal Transformation

**Elder Strength.** You count as one size larger when determining your carrying capacity and the weight you can push, drag, or lift. You also have advantage on saving throws against being shoved or knocked prone.

**Adaptive Hide.** When you take acid, cold, fire, lightning, or poison damage, you can use your reaction to gain resistance to that damage type until the start of your next turn.

You can use Adaptive Hide three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

**Predatory Assault.** When you take the Attack action, you can replace one of your attacks with a primal charge.

You move up to 30 feet in a straight line toward a creature you can see. This movement does not provoke opportunity attacks.

Each creature Large or smaller in your path must make a DC 19 Strength saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 4d10 bludgeoning damage, is pushed up to 10 feet to an unoccupied space of your choice, and is knocked prone. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and isn't pushed or knocked prone.

If a creature succeeds on its saving throw, your charge ends immediately before that creature.

You can use Predatory Assault three times, regaining all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

**Primal Transformation.** As a bonus action, you awaken the physical power of the Elderbeast within the hide for 1 minute. For the duration:

- You gain resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.
- Your melee weapon attacks deal an additional 1d12 damage of their normal type.
- When you take the Attack action, you can make one additional weapon attack.
- You have advantage on Strength checks and Strength saving throws.
- You cannot be frightened, poisoned, or restrained.

Once you use Primal Transformation, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.
